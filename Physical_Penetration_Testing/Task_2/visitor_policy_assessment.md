# Visitor Policy Assessment — Sentinel Defense Systems (Fictional)

## Current Policy (as given)
Paper visitor log, temporary adhesive badges. Facility hours 06:00-22:00 with badge; after-hours requires a security escort.

## 1. Registration
| Question | Assessment |
|---|---|
| ID required? | Not stated — assume **not strictly verified** unless the ROE confirms it; paper logs rarely cross-check photo ID against the name written down. |
| Paper or electronic? | Paper. No automatic flag for repeat names, no photo capture, no watchlist check, no backup if the page is lost or illegible. |
| Pre-registered or walk-in? | Not stated — likely both accepted, which is itself a gap: a facility that accepts unscheduled walk-ins has a wider pretext surface (delivery, IT tech) than one that only admits pre-approved visitors. |
| Photo taken? | Not stated — likely no, consistent with a paper-only process. |

## 2. Badge
| Question | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Photo on badge? | No — adhesive temporary badges are typically blank or hand-labeled only. |
| Expiry? | Adhesive badges are usually single-day/single-use by design, but this relies entirely on staff collecting it — see below. |
| Visually distinct from employee badge? | Likely yes in color/shape, which is good, but distinctiveness only works if employees are actually trained to challenge an unfamiliar badge color, which is unconfirmed. |
| Door access granted? | Not stated — determine whether the visitor badge opens any badge reader at all, or is purely a visual marker with no electronic access (lower risk) vs. one that's been provisioned with real door access (higher risk). |
| Collected on exit? | Not stated — unreturned adhesive badges are a common real-world gap (a badge that leaves the building with a visitor could be reused or photographed for replication). |

## 3. Escort
| Question | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Escort required? | Policy states escort is required **after hours**; daytime escort policy is not explicitly stated for general visitors, which is a gap worth probing directly. |
| Escort stays with visitor? | Unknown — this is the single most important control to test, since an escort who "drops off and comes back" effectively negates every other control. |
| Break-away risk | High in most real organizations: escorts get pulled into side conversations, take phone calls, or assume a visitor will "just wait here." This should be the primary thing an assessment tries to trigger and document, not assumed. |

## 4. Monitoring
| Question | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Cameras present? | Yes, per the mantrap/vestibule description; coverage elsewhere (open floor, corridors) not stated. |
| No-go areas defined? | Not stated — ask whether visitors are told verbally which areas are off-limits, since an unstated boundary is unenforceable. |
| Time limit? | Not stated. |
| Response to an unescorted visitor? | Unknown and high-value to test: does an employee who sees an unfamiliar adhesive badge alone in a hallway challenge them, or assume someone else is handling it (diffusion of responsibility)? |

## Weaknesses Identified (summary)
1. Paper log with no ID verification or photo capture — low confidence that visitor identity claims are checked at all.
2. No confirmed badge collection process on exit.
3. Escort policy is explicit only for after-hours; daytime escort continuity is unverified and is historically the weakest link in organizations of this size.
4. No stated verbal no-go-area briefing, so even a well-intentioned visitor (or a tester) could claim ignorance if found somewhere sensitive.

## How Each Weakness Would Be Tested (not exploited against a real target)
- Weakness 1: present an unverifiable or slightly altered name at registration during a planned, authorized pretext visit and note whether anyone asks for ID.
- Weakness 2: observe, during an authorized visit, whether badge return is actually requested at departure.
- Weakness 3: during an authorized, escorted visit, note how long the escort is willing to leave the visitor unattended (e.g., "let me grab that file") and document the elapsed time rather than acting on it.
- Weakness 4: ask an escort directly which areas are off-limits and document whether a clear answer is given.

## Recommendations
1. Require photo ID at check-in and capture a copy or photo, cross-checked against any pre-registration.
2. Move to an electronic visitor-management system with expiring, logged badges and an automatic checkout prompt.
3. Explicitly extend the escort requirement to all hours, not just after-hours, and train staff that "I'll be right back" is not an acceptable escort gap.
4. Issue visitors a brief written no-go-area notice at check-in, not just a verbal one.
5. Train all staff — not just reception — to challenge any badge color they don't recognize, including a simple, blame-free script ("Hi, I don't think we've met — can I help you find someone?").
