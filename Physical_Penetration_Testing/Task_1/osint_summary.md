## Five OSINT Sources and Intelligence Each Would Provide
| # | Source | Intelligence Gained |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Google Maps satellite view** | Building footprint, number of buildings, roof access points, parking layout, loading dock location, perimeter fencing, natural cover (tree lines, neighboring lots), distance from public roads/sidewalks for legal observation. |
| 2 | **Google Street View** | Entrance door types (glass vs. solid, manual vs. automatic), visible badge readers, camera housings and approximate aim, security signage ("Visitors must check in," alarm company stickers), bike racks/smoking areas near doors. |
| 3 | **LinkedIn** | Org chart and departments (reveals who works near the data center/server room, who is in facilities/security — valuable pretext targets or people to avoid impersonating), employee tenure, recent hires who may not recognize staff yet, headshots for later "do I know you" conversations (used only to inform pretext plausibility, not for impersonation of a named individual). |
| 4 | **Job postings** (company site, Indeed, LinkedIn Jobs) | Badge/access-control vendor and VMS (e.g. a posting asking for "Lenel OnGuard experience" names the badge system), alarm company, help-desk ticketing tool, whether they have an in-house receptionist vs. a lobby kiosk, open security-guard or facilities postings revealing contractor name. |
| 5 | **County property/permit records** | Building owner vs. tenant, square footage, fire-marshal occupancy load, any building-permit filings showing recent access-control or camera installs (dates help estimate how current the system is). |
| 6 (bonus) | **Company website "About/Careers" photos, Glassdoor reviews, Google Reviews** | Interior photos sometimes show badge readers, server-room doors, break rooms; Glassdoor comments about "the guard who's always on his phone" or "we never badge the side door" are high-value social-proof of real control gaps. |

## Employees to Consider for Social-Engineering Pretext Planning
*(Listed for awareness of role-based exposure and to plan **authorized, client-approved** pretexts only — not as a target list for actual contact without sign-off.)*

| Role (not named individual) | Why relevant |
|---|---|
| Front-desk/reception or office manager | First point of contact for a visitor/delivery pretext; controls visitor badge issuance — understanding their process reveals the easiest and hardest ways in. |
| Facilities/building maintenance | Often holds master keys, knows guard schedule and alarm codes; a "contractor" pretext naturally interacts with this role, so it's useful to know it exists and is distinct from security. |
| New hires (<3 months tenure, found via LinkedIn) | Less likely to recognize coworkers or question an unfamiliar face; higher tailgating/politeness risk. Not to be singled out for deception beyond what the ROE authorizes. |
| IT/helpdesk | Possible vector for a "badge not working, can you let me in" pretext; also the group a cloned badge would need to avoid. |

**Note:** Any pretext involving a specific named employee requires it to be listed and approved in the Rules of Engagement before use, and must avoid causing personal harm, embarrassment, or lasting distress to that individual.
