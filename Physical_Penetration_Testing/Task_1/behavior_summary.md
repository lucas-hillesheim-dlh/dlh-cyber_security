# Employee Behavior Summary — Meridian Technologies (Simulated)

## Arrival / Departure Patterns
- Peak arrival: 07:45–09:00 at EP-1 (lobby) and EP-2 (rear employee door); peak departure 17:00–18:00, mirrored.
- Rear door (EP-2) carries the bulk of regular-employee traffic since it leads directly to the employee lot — visitors and deliveries are channelled to EP-1/EP-3 instead, meaning EP-2 sees the least varied (most habitual, least scrutinizing) foot traffic.

## Badge Use and Tailgating
- EP-1 (lobby): receptionist present reduces tailgating, but queueing at peak times creates brief windows where a badge is shown but not verified against a face.
- EP-2 (rear): no human monitor; workplace courtesy ("door-holding") is the primary tailgate vector, especially for someone carrying something (boxes, coffee trays) that invites a held door.
- EP-3 (dock): personnel door tailgating likely during scheduled delivery windows when staff are focused on the truck, not the door.

## Smoking Areas / Breaks
- Likely smoking/break area near EP-3 (dock) based on typical office layouts placing it away from the main entrance; staff stepping out briefly may prop the personnel door rather than badge back in — a recurring, predictable soft spot.

## Lunch
- Many staff likely leave campus or use the Annex break area around midday; this period overlaps with the guard's 12:00–12:29 lunch, compounding the gap identified in the patrol timeline (more foot traffic, less guard presence, same window).

## Dress Code / Blending In
- Software company, mid-size — business-casual is a safe assumption for a pretext (avoid suit-and-tie, which could stand out as "sales visitor" and draw more scrutiny at reception; avoid overly casual too).

## Deliveries / Visitor Process
- Deliveries route to EP-3 dock; visitors route to EP-1 lobby for sign-in. A delivery pretext bypasses the visitor sign-in process entirely, which is a meaningful difference in scrutiny level and worth highlighting as a vulnerability.
