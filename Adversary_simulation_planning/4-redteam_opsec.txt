Red Team OpSec Assessment

Part A - OpSec Risk Table:

| Risk                                 | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation                                                                                                   |
|---------------------------------------|------------|--------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| C2 infrastructure exposure            | Medium     | High   | Register and host all C2 infrastructure under dedicated operational accounts/business identities, never personal or corporate-identifiable ones; use domain fronting or redirectors, categorize domains ahead of time, and keep WHOIS/registration data privacy-protected and unlinked to any operator. |
| Operator identity leakage             | Medium     | High   | Use operator-specific pseudonymous accounts and burner credentials for all infrastructure registration, payment, and tooling; never reuse personal email, phone numbers, payment methods, or social accounts for engagement work; enforce this via a pre-engagement checklist sign-off. |
| Tool signature detection              | High       | Medium | Custom-compile and obfuscate payloads per engagement rather than reusing public/default builds; test tooling against representative AV/EDR in a lab before deployment; avoid default C2 framework profiles and indicators (ports, URIs, user-agents, certificates). |
| Log artifacts left on target systems  | High       | Medium | Track every action taken on target systems in a live operator log; execute only the minimum steps needed to prove the technique; clean up created files, registry keys, scheduled tasks, and accounts immediately after each step; verify cleanup before closing out the engagement. |

Part B - Pre-Engagement OpSec Checklist:

Communication Security
[ ] All team coordination (planning, live status updates, findings) happens over an encrypted, access-controlled channel (e.g. Signal, encrypted team chat) — never unencrypted email or personal messaging apps.
[ ] Access to the coordination channel and any shared operational documents is restricted to engagement team members only, with access revoked at engagement close.

Infrastructure Setup
[ ] C2 servers, redirectors, and domains are provisioned fresh for the engagement under operational (non-personal) identities and payment methods, never reused from prior engagements or personal infrastructure.
[ ] Redirectors sit between target-facing traffic and the actual C2 server, with logging/alerting configured so the team is notified of unexpected inbound connections.

Tool Preparation
[ ] Payloads and implants are custom-compiled per engagement with unique indicators (hashes, strings, network signatures) rather than reused from previous ops or taken directly from public repositories.
[ ] Tooling is validated against a representative AV/EDR baseline in a lab prior to use, to confirm expected detection/evasion behavior and avoid surprises mid-engagement.

Documentation Hygiene
[ ] No client data, credentials, screenshots, or engagement notes are stored on personal devices or personal cloud accounts; all artifacts stay on company-managed, encrypted systems.
[ ] All engagement documentation (scope, RoE, logs, findings) is stored in an access-controlled repository and permanently deleted or archived per the client's data-retention agreement at engagement close.

Part C - Why Red Team OpSec Protects the Client:

Red team OpSec is not just about keeping the operators safe or hidden, it is
what makes the exercise's results trustworthy in the first place. If the
operation is exposed early through sloppy OpSec, the Blue Team stops
reacting to a realistic adversary and starts reacting to a known test,
which invalidates the exercise's ability to measure real detection and
response capability. A poorly secured operation can also leak the client's
own sensitive data, infrastructure details, or the red team's methodology
to unintended parties, creating real exposure the client never agreed to
accept. Ultimately, the client is paying for an honest, controlled
simulation of risk, and maintaining strong OpSec is how the red team
honors that trust and keeps the engagement safe, legal, and valid.
